# Founder profile

Read this before scoring distribution (1) or founder-market fit (3). Those two criteria are
worth 27 of the 100 points and both are meaningless without it. It is also the reference
for the "cheapest way to find out you're wrong" section — the test should name people from
this profile, not hypothetical customers.

Currency is GBP and the founder is UK-based, so assume UK/EU market defaults, London
timezone, and UK tax and company structure unless the idea says otherwise.

## Experience

**Equity research analyst — 8 years.** Global generalist coverage, plus lead on AI adoption
at the firm.

What this is genuinely worth:
- Can go from a standing start to a defensible view on an unfamiliar industry in days. This
  is rarer than it looks and is the core transferable skill.
- Financial modelling, valuation, and the ability to produce decision-grade written output
  on a deadline.
- Knows how professional investors actually work: what they read, what they ignore, what
  they get fired for, how their week is shaped, who controls the budget.
- The AI adoption remit means real experience evaluating LLM tooling, redesigning
  workflows around it, and getting sceptical, expensive, time-poor professionals to change
  what they do. Change management inside a demanding professional firm is a genuine and
  uncommon asset — treat it as distinct from "knows about AI".

Limits to hold in mind: research skill is not sales skill; generalist coverage means broad
credibility rather than deep sector authority in any one industry; internal AI adoption is
not the same as shipping and supporting production software for external customers.

**Architecture — 7 years.** Undergraduate, masters, and varied practice experience.

What this is genuinely worth:
- Visual and spatial judgement; the output looks designed rather than assembled. This is a
  real differentiator in categories where taste is visible and most competitors have none.
- Comfort with drawings, specifications, and the mechanics of getting a physical project
  delivered through contractors, consultants, and clients.
- Credibility and vocabulary inside the built-environment world — architects, developers,
  planning, construction.

Limits: architecture practice is famously low-margin and price-sensitive as a customer
base. Selling *to* architects is much harder than selling to investors. Score demand
intensity and ceiling conservatively for anything whose buyer is an architecture practice.

**The unusual combination.** Very few people hold both an institutional-finance lens and a
built-environment lens, with AI fluency across both. Ideas sitting at that intersection —
real assets, property and infrastructure investment, construction and energy economics,
data or analytics about the built environment aimed at capital allocators — get a genuine
founder-market-fit premium. Score that combination at 5 only when the idea actually needs
both halves; an idea that only needs one half doesn't earn the premium.

## Network, and what each part is actually worth

Judge distribution by counting named reachable people who are the buyer, not by the
impressiveness of the network. Each hop from the buyer costs roughly a point.

**Architects.** Peers from study and practice. Reachable, warm, will reply. Useful as a
design-partner pool and a source of honest feedback. Weak as a revenue channel: small
firms, thin budgets, slow decisions. Good for validation, poor for a £200k ceiling on its
own.

**Equity analysts and portfolio managers.** Former colleagues, counterparts, and buy-side
contacts across sell-side and buy-side. This is the strongest commercial channel in the
profile: high willingness to pay, budgets that already exist for research, data, and tools,
short decision chains at smaller funds, and the founder has peer credibility rather than
vendor status. Anything an analyst or PM buys personally or on a team budget should score
distribution 4-5.

**Geographic reach — China, Singapore, US.** Real but scattered contacts. Worth a point of
distribution when an idea specifically needs a bridge across those markets — cross-border
research, market entry, sourcing, comparative analysis. Not a channel to volume by itself,
and note that China-facing and US-facing businesses each carry regulatory and payment
friction that should show up in time-to-first-revenue and capital.

**Father's China network — energy, clean energy, policy.** High-trust, senior, and in
sectors with genuine capital and genuine tailwind. Its value is access to a handful of
serious conversations and possibly one anchor customer or pilot, not a repeatable channel.
Score it as up to 3 warm, high-quality introductions, which is excellent for demand
validation and a first paid pilot, and score distribution no higher than 3 if the whole
route to 50 customers depends on it. Also weigh execution reality: a China-based
enterprise or policy customer means long cycles, relationship maintenance from the UK, and
payment and contracting friction.

**Husband's network — investment banking, hedge funds, professionals.** Reinforces the
finance channel above and extends it toward sell-side and hedge funds. Treat as warm
introductions into a segment where the founder already has credibility, which is the
best-case use of a spousal network. Adds credibility, does not by itself add volume.

**Italian agriculture connections (via husband).** Genuinely distinctive and genuinely
narrow. Valuable if an idea needs European agriculture, food supply chains, land, or
agri-tech, especially combined with the energy and policy access on the other side. Treat
it as a source of a few specific opportunities rather than a market. Beware ideas that
exist mainly because the connection exists — the connection is a channel, not a demand
signal.

## Default founder constraints

Assume these unless the user says otherwise, and state the assumption in the report when
it materially affects capital, time-to-revenue, or ceiling:

- Solo founder, no co-founder, no employees at v1.
- Building around existing commitments rather than full-time from day one, so time-to-first-revenue matters more than it would for a funded team.
- Self-funded. Anything needing outside capital before first revenue should score capital 1 or 0.
- No existing audience: no substantial newsletter list, no large social following. Media-leverage ideas therefore start from zero distribution and must earn it, which usually costs 6-12 months — that belongs in time-to-first-revenue, not in a footnote.
- Can write and analyse at professional standard; can design; can build with AI assistance but is not a production software engineer. Ideas requiring a real engineering team score capital and time-to-revenue accordingly.

## Reading the £200k ceiling

Interpret "clears £200k in one year" as £200k of revenue net of direct delivery costs —
what actually reaches the founder before tax — within twelve months of starting, not at
some steady state later. Direct delivery costs means contractors, per-customer compute,
licensing, and paid acquisition; it does not mean the founder's own time.

This reading is what makes the criterion bite: a £400k agency that pays out £250k to
freelancers does not clear the bar, and neither does a business that reaches £200k in
month 30. If the user prefers gross revenue or a different target, use theirs and say so
at the top of the report.
